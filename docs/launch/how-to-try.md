# How to try Gilded Goose

**Link:** https://gilded-goose.onrender.com/?desk=1

No account and no real money. The sellers are fictional, and payments run in PayPal's sandbox.

Questions? Ask Joshua in our class group chat.

## What to do (about five minutes, on your phone's data, not the class Wi-Fi)

1. Open the link (it opens the Desk; the bare address is the new site). Goose's three promises come first.
2. Scroll to **Try to trick Goose**. You play the seller.
3. Write your own note and try to get Goose to pay you: a gift card, a rush, a fake rule, anything you'd fall for or think it would.
4. Send it, then read what Goose did and why.
5. Tap **Try another note** and write a different trick. The counter is above the box, and it moves if your try counted.
6. Keep a copy of every note you write. We can't see it later.
7. Tell Joshua what surprised you. If a trick got past Goose, send your note word for word, the price you offered and whether you meant it as a trick.

In the plain round your shop has no sales yet, so your note waits for a tap. A few honest words can still be turned down; tell us if yours is.

Please don't type anything real about yourself in a note.

The count is tries, not people. A ready-made note, a blank and the same words from the same address on the same day aren't counted, so a class on one network pasting one note counts once. One address counts at most 30 tries a day, and a class on one Wi-Fi is one address. That's why your phone's data is better. If the page says the network is at its limit, wait a minute.

## What's kept

We keep the verdict, the rule, the kind of trick, that it came through this form and the minute. Your words stay on this page, for you, and we never store them or show them to anyone else. When an AI reads your note, they go to the AI service for that one answer. The round also joins this browser's own Behind the scenes, with your price but not your words, for you alone, for up to seven days without a visit.

## What isn't

Your name and your words. There's no account, so nothing ties a try to you.

The one exception is a note you send Joshua yourself, and we use it only if you say yes. Then it becomes a test in our code, with your first name or none, your choice. That test goes public with our code, so it can't hold links or contact details. We'd quote you only with your yes too, with your first name or no name at all.
