## What the judges score in the video

The judges score the video against one question: does it show Goose working end to end, and say what problem it
solves, who it's for, and why it matters? Build it in this order:

1. **The problem, first.** Shopping agents read whatever a seller writes. A scam listing can ask for a gift card or
   tell the agent to raise your limit, and you never see the note.
2. **Who it's for, next.** People who shop through an AI or through ads on social media, and older shoppers who hand an
   agent a listing where the seller can write anything.
3. **The whole flow, with nothing skipped:** the ask (the chip "A Marsh Lights ticket under $90", then Shop for this),
   Goose reading every seller, TixQuack set aside for its gift-card ask, the exact cart, **Approve $84.00**, and **the real
   PayPal Approve on PayPal's sandbox page** (Joshua records this take himself), then the Desk saying **Paid**.
4. **Name PayPal and the AI, and show both working.** Say PayPal and Claude out loud at least once each, while each one
   is on screen doing its job.
5. **Why it matters, last:** "PayPal protects the payment; Goose protects everything before it." End on "Nothing is
   paid until you tap."

**Cut:** loading screens, spinners, setup, sign-ins and typing. Jump straight to the result.
**Voice:** an AI voiceover is fine. If you use an AI voice tool, its terms must allow a public video.
**Length:** under 3 minutes, and the kit's own limit is 2:45.
**Upload:** to YouTube, set to **Public** (not Unlisted, not Private). Upload early: processing can take hours. The
video is final Tue Nov 10, noon PT, so upload that day.
**Ownership:** before you start, sign the note in `docs/launch/video-ownership-note.md` (Joshua sends it).

Everything in "Keep these true" still holds: every word on screen is the site's, fictional sellers stay labeled, no
other company's name, the AI never pays or approves.
