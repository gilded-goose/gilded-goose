# Gilded Goose: the video brief (one page)

**Gilded Goose is an AI shopper that can't be fooled into paying.** The video tells it in seven parts, in this order.
The rest of this kit (`README.md`) has the takes, the steps on screen and the setup; this page is the story.

**The rules.** Upload to YouTube set to **Public**. Under 3 minutes; the kit's own limit is 2:45. No music. An AI
voiceover is fine, if its terms allow a public video. **No brands:** no other company's name or mark, in words or on
screen. PayPal and Claude are the exception: the organizers' rule says to name both and show them working, so keep them.
Every quoted line below is the site's own and has its row in the team's claims check; say it as written.

## 1. The problem
Shopping agents read whatever a seller writes. A scam listing can ask for a gift card or tell the agent to raise your
limit, and you never see the note. On the end card as type, never spoken:
"In FTC reports from 2018 to 2021, about one in four people who lost money to fraud paid with a gift card."

## 2. Who it's for
People who shop through an AI or through ads on social media, and older shoppers who hand an agent a listing where the
seller can write anything.

## 3. Why now
AI that shops for you is arriving, and people are deciding whether to trust it with a purchase. Goose's answer: let it
shop, never let it pay. "Goose shops for you. Nothing is paid until you tap."

## 4. The four moments: A Week with Goose (the spine)
One week of shopping in the practice market, four moments, said in the voice in this order:
- "Day 1: You ask Goose for a Marsh Lights ticket under $90."
- "Day 2: The cheapest seller, TixQuack, asks $55 by gift card, so Goose sets it aside and tells you why."
- "Day 5: Lantern Hall Box Office's total falls to $84, under your $90, so Goose shows you the deal."
- "Day 5: You tap to pay $84 in PayPal; nothing but your tap could pay."

Footnote, on screen: "These moments come from our practice market. Prices are simulated."
**Claude, shown working:** over day 1. Claude reads your words into the ask (**Here's what I understood.**), and code
checks them; Claude never holds your limit, a PayPal tool or the Approve button.

## 5. The ping
Only if the README's section on beat 3's version names the real ping; otherwise beat 3's fallback, with no ping.
The real browser notification, recorded by hand on desktop Chrome, whole screen (the setup is in `README.md`, under beat 3). Never
faked: if it doesn't land, film beat 3's fallback instead. In one take:
1. On the landing's ticket, say "Missed the drop? Set your price." and press **Set your price**. It opens your list,
   where the Marsh Lights ticket's price reads 90. Press **Set your price** there; Chrome asks, press **Allow**.
2. A few seconds later the notification lands. Say nothing over it. It reads **Goose · simulated price alert**, then
   "Simulated day 5: Marsh Lights ticket is $84, under your $90 price. Tap to review."
3. Click it: Goose opens **Here's what I understood.**, held to your $90 price. The deal isn't on screen yet.
4. Press **Shop for this**. Goose checks the sellers, and **Approve $84.00** comes into view. Stop. Don't press it.

The ping finds the deal; it never buys: "The ping never buys: only your tap pays."

## 6. The tap
Joshua's own take: he presses "Approve $84.00", approves on PayPal's own sandbox page, and the site says
**Paid $84.00**. **PayPal, shown working:** say its name here, over its own page. The promise the tap proves:
"Goose can't pay. Only your tap in PayPal does. The Watcher can miss a scam, so you see the exact cart first."

## 7. The close: our view, then why now
Say first "Our view of what's next, not a feature.", then:
"AI is moving into rings and earbuds, where there's no screen to check. That's when a shopper that can't be fooled into paying matters most."
"Even there, we'd still send you the exact cart, on a screen you trust like your phone, before your tap."
"The next era runs on approvals."
"We think every AI that acts for you will soon ask for your yes, and a yes is only safe if what you approve is exactly what you asked for."
End on: "Goose reads every seller, watches the price and shows you the deal when it's right, and your one tap in PayPal is the only thing that pays."
